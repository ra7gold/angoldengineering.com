SEC 02

Selected Work

AI pit detection for dried cherries

California dried-fruit processor
Scope
Ground-up development of an ML detection system for pits in dried cherries, integrated with multi-robot removal on a live, high-speed sorting line — model development, deployment, and production support.
Problem
A single pit in a finished bag is a food-safety liability, and no commercial detection system existed for pits in dried cherries — wrinkled, highly variable product screened at full line speed.
Outcome
A first-of-its-kind detection system, developed by Angold Engineering and running in commercial production since 2020. Continuous model and process improvement since — including tracing a silent software fault that was discarding roughly 40% of valid robot targets, fixed and verified on the running line. Ongoing production support, including off-hours response.

GNSS guidance & QC for pile-driving fleets

Utility-scale solar contractor
Scope
Centimeter-grade positioning, operator guidance, and as-built QC for pile-driving equipment across utility-scale solar sites in three states.
Problem
Construction tolerances tighter than the workflows built to hit them — with each site on its own local datum that no off-the-shelf GPS setting understands.
Outcome
Positioning tied correctly to each client site calibration rather than published control, a repeatable RTK base-station recipe deployed fleet-wide, remote fleet connectivity, and a field app operators use daily. On-machine sensor fusion work continuing.

Process control for a complex pasteurization line

Food processor
Scope
Process control and HMI modernization for a complex, multi-stage pasteurization line.
Outcome
A modern operator interface on legacy process equipment, with remote diagnostics built in so problems get solved without waiting for a site visit.

SEC 04

About

Hard problems, cool people. That’s the filter every project passes through.

Angold Engineering is led by Russ Angold, a licensed Professional Mechanical Engineer building at the intersection of agriculture, AI, and hardware. His AI defect-detection system has run in commercial production at a California food processor since 2020, with parallel R&D in specialty-crop harvesting automation and a universal AI-trainable sorting platform.

The firm is deliberately small: the engineer who scopes your project is the one who designs it, commissions it, and answers the phone when it breaks. The through-line in the work is production reality — plenty of engineering looks finished in CAD and falls apart on the plant floor or in the field. Angold Engineering's projects are judged where yours are: on uptime, throughput, and whether the crew actually uses the thing.
